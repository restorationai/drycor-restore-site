**TL;DR:** In Florida, water cleanup typically costs $1,200 to $7,500, with most single-room jobs landing between $1,200 and $3,000 and whole-floor or contaminated-water jobs running $6,000 or more. The biggest cost drivers are the water's category (clean, gray, or black), how many square feet are affected, and how long the structure sat wet before drying started.

If you've got standing water from a burst pipe, an overflowing washing machine, or a slow leak that finally gave out, you're probably staring at wet flooring and wondering what this is going to cost before you even call anyone. The honest answer depends less on the size of the puddle and more on what kind of water it is and how fast you act. Here's what actually drives the number in Florida, and what a realistic bill looks like.

## How Much Does Water Cleanup Cost in FL?

Most Florida homeowners pay between $1,200 and $7,500 for professional water cleanup, with the price scaling to the square footage affected and the water category involved. A single contained room with clean water usually sits at the low end; multiple rooms or contaminated water push it toward the high end.

These figures cover extraction, moisture mapping, and structural drying, which is what most people mean by "water cleanup." Full water damage restoration, which adds repairs, flooring replacement, and reconstruction, is a separate and larger number (our [water damage restoration cost guide](/blog/water-damage-restoration-cost-fl/) breaks that down).

## What Drives the Cost of Water Cleanup?

The water's category, measured against the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), is the single biggest factor in the price. Category 1 (clean water, like a supply line break) is the cheapest to remediate. Category 2 (gray water, like a washing machine discharge) and Category 3 (black water, like sewage or storm surge) require more aggressive removal of porous materials and antimicrobial treatment, which raises both labor and material costs.

Square footage and how many rooms are affected matter almost as much. A single bedroom dries out differently than a connected living room, kitchen, and hallway, each needing its own air movers and dehumidifiers running for days.

Response time changes the bill too. Water that sits for 48 hours soaks deeper into drywall, subfloor, and cabinetry, meaning more material has to come out instead of just drying in place. Florida's humidity doesn't help: ambient moisture in the air slows evaporation, so equipment often has to run longer here than in a drier climate.

Finally, what's underneath the water matters. Tile over concrete slab dries faster and cheaper than carpet and pad, which usually have to be pulled. Hardwood and engineered flooring can sometimes be saved with specialty drying mats, but that adds equipment cost.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Cleanup?

Most Florida homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a pipe that bursts or an appliance hose that fails without warning, but they typically exclude gradual leaks and flood water. Coverage depends heavily on the water source, so this is the first thing an adjuster will ask about.

A slow leak behind a wall that's been dripping for months is usually treated as a maintenance issue, not a covered loss, per the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). Flood water, meaning water that enters from outside the home (storm surge, a swollen creek, rising groundwater), is excluded from standard homeowners policies entirely and requires a separate flood policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.floodsmart.gov/) or a private flood carrier. Sewage and sump pump backups also aren't covered unless the policy has a specific water backup endorsement added.

Documentation protects the claim either way. Photograph the damage before anything is moved, keep every receipt, and get a written scope of work from whoever does the drying. ## What Happens During Professional Water Cleanup?

Professional water cleanup follows a set sequence: assessment and category classification, water extraction, moisture mapping, equipment placement, monitored drying, and a final moisture check before the job is signed off. Skipping steps is usually what leads to hidden mold later.

1. **Assessment and category classification.** A technician identifies the water source, classifies the category, and checks how far moisture has traveled behind walls and under flooring using a moisture meter.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water comes out first, using truck-mounted or portable extraction units, before any drying equipment goes in.
3. **Moisture mapping and equipment placement.** Air movers and dehumidifiers get positioned based on the moisture readings, not just the visible wet area.
4. **Monitored drying.** Equipment typically runs 3 to 5 days, with daily moisture checks to confirm materials are actually drying, not just sitting under a fan.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment (if needed).** Category 2 or 3 water, or any drying that's taken longer than 48 hours, usually gets an antimicrobial application to limit mold growth, since mold can start within 24 to 48 hours on wet materials per the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).
6. **Final moisture check.** A pass/fail reading on every affected material before equipment comes out and the area is cleared for repairs.
