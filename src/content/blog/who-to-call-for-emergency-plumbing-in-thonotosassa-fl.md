Not every plumbing emergency needs the same call, though. If water is actively spreading into drywall, flooring, or ceilings, you need a restoration company that can stop the water AND start drying before mold sets in. If the problem is contained to a fixture (a clogged toilet, a dripping faucet, a slow leak under a sink with no standing water yet), a standard plumber can usually handle it without a mitigation crew.

## When Do You Call a Restoration Company vs. a Plumber in Thonotosassa?

Call a restoration company first when water has already reached flooring, drywall, insulation, or your ceiling. At that point the clock on mold growth matters more than the repair itself, and a straight plumber won't bring extraction equipment or dehumidifiers.

A lot of Thonotosassa properties sit on private wells and septic systems rather than county water and sewer lines, especially outside the denser pockets near Fowler Avenue and Lake Thonotosassa. That changes the triage a little: a failed well pressure tank floods a utility closet, not a street main, and a septic backup means sewage cleanup, not just a water call. Either way, once water or sewage is on the floor, mitigation needs to start within hours, not days, to protect subflooring and drywall.

Call a licensed plumber (not a restoration company) when the issue is isolated to the fixture itself and no water has escaped: a running toilet, a slow drip, a garbage disposal jam. Call 911 or your electric utility immediately, before calling anyone else, if water is near an outlet, a breaker panel, or any visible wiring. Call your insurance carrier after mitigation starts, not before. Most adjusters want to see that water was stopped and drying began promptly, and documentation from a mitigation crew helps the claim rather than complicates it.

## What Happens When You Call for a Plumbing Emergency?

The first call is mostly triage: where the water is coming from, whether the shutoff valve has been turned, and how long water has been running. Have your address, the approximate source (water heater, supply line, toilet, washing machine hose), and whether anyone has already shut off the main water supply.

Once a crew arrives, the first steps are usually the same regardless of the source: locate and stop the active water, assess what's already saturated, and pull out standing water with extraction equipment before it migrates further into walls or subfloor. Moisture meters get used right away to map how far the water has traveled, since damage is often wider than what's visible on the surface.

After the water is stopped and extracted, drying equipment (air movers, dehumidifiers) stays in place for several days, with moisture readings checked periodically until materials are back to a dry standard. If a pipe or fixture actually needs replacing, that repair work can run alongside the drying, or afterward, depending on what failed. ## What Should You Do Before Help Arrives?

Shut off the water at the main valve or the fixture's local shutoff if you can reach it safely. This is the single most useful thing a homeowner can do before any crew arrives, since every additional minute of flow adds to both the damage and the eventual bill.

Move anything you can lift (rugs, furniture legs, boxes) off the wet floor, and avoid running a shop vac over water near an outlet. If the water involves a backed-up drain or toilet, treat it as contaminated: skip wading through it and keep pets and kids out of the room. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) notes that sudden, accidental water damage is typically covered under a standard homeowners policy, while damage from neglect usually isn't, which is part of why documenting the timeline matters from the first phone call.

If you're not sure whether what you're looking at is a plumbing fix or a restoration job, describe what you see on the call and let the crew make that call. Getting the wrong type of help delays the right one.

If you're already past the point of a quick fixture repair, the faster next step is to [get a water damage estimate](/contact/) rather than wait to see if it dries on its own.
