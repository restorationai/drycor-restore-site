## When Do You Call a Mold Remediation Company Instead of a Handyman?

Call a remediation company once the mold growth is bigger than about 10 square feet, roughly a 3-by-3-foot patch, or if you can't identify and fix the moisture source yourself. Smaller spots on a hard, non-porous surface (tile, glass, sealed countertop) can sometimes be wiped down with soap and water by the homeowner.

Anything on drywall, insulation, carpet, or wood framing is different. Mold on porous material usually means the material has to come out, not just get wiped. The [EPA notes that mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold) on a wet surface, so a small patch found early can often stay small if someone fixes the moisture source fast.

If the mold is tied to an active leak, a failed AC condensate line, or standing water, call a restoration company first, not a painter. Painting over active mold growth only hides it. If you smell gas, see exposed wiring near the damp area, or the ceiling is sagging, that's a safety call to the fire department or your utility company before anyone touches the mold.

## What Happens When You Call a Mold Remediation Company?

The first call is mostly information gathering: where the growth is, how big it looks, how long it's been there, and whether there's an active water source. Have your address, a rough size estimate, and photos ready if you can get them.

On arrival, a technician inspects the affected area with a moisture meter and usually a borescope for wall cavities, checking how far the moisture has traveled behind the visible growth. They'll isolate the work area with poly sheeting and set the space to negative air pressure with a HEPA air scrubber before removing anything, which keeps spores from spreading to the rest of the home. This containment-first approach follows the [ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for professional mold remediation](https://iicrc.org/s520/).

Damaged porous material (drywall, insulation, baseboard) typically gets removed and bagged rather than treated in place. Framing and other structural wood get cleaned, treated, and dried. The crew should hand you a written scope before starting, not an estimate scribbled on a business card.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover the Call?

Most standard homeowners policies cover mold remediation only when the mold resulted from a sudden, accidental covered loss, like a burst supply line, not from a slow leak or long-term humidity. Coverage for mold is also commonly capped at a set dollar amount even when it is included, according to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance).

That's one more reason documentation matters from the first visit: photos, moisture readings, and a written scope of work give your adjuster something concrete to evaluate.
