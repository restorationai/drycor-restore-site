## How do you verify a contractor's Florida license before you sign anything?

You can confirm any Florida contractor's license status for free through the state's own lookup tool before you let anyone on your roof. Search the company's license number or business name at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/), the Department of Business and Professional Regulation's public portal, and check that the license is active, not expired or under disciplinary action.

Every legitimate Florida general contractor has a number in the format of a certified building contractor license, similar to DRYCOR RESTORE's CBC1253966. If a company that showed up after the storm can't give you a license number on request, or the number comes back inactive, that's a hard stop. The same lookup shows you the license type, which matters: storm rebuild work (roof decking, framing, structural repairs) requires a certified contractor license, not just a handyman registration.

## What warning signs point to an out-of-state storm chaser?

The biggest warning sign is pressure to sign an Assignment of Benefits (AOB) form before an adjuster has inspected the damage or you've gotten a written scope of work. An AOB signs your insurance claim rights over to the contractor, and once it's signed, you often lose the ability to negotiate directly with your own insurer.

Other signs to watch for: out-of-state plates on the truck, a business card with no permanent local address, demands for full payment upfront, and a hard sales pitch to sign something on the spot. Florida tightened AOB rules in its 2023 insurance reform legislation specifically because of storm-chasing abuse, and homeowners who have questions about a contractor or an insurance practice can check with the [Florida Department of Financial Services' consumer division](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers) before signing anything. A company that's still going to be in Thonotosassa next year has no reason to rush you.
