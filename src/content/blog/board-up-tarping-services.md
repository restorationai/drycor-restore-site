## What Does a Board-Up and Tarping Service Actually Do?

A board-up and tarping service seals broken windows, doors, and roof openings with exterior-grade plywood and reinforced poly tarping to stop wind-driven rain, pests, and trespassers from reaching the interior while permanent repairs get scheduled.

For openings at ground level, that usually means 1/2-inch or 5/8-inch CDX plywood cut to fit and screwed (not nailed) directly into the framing, not just the trim, so it holds through wind gusts. For roof damage, crews use heavy-gauge poly tarping rated for extended UV and rain exposure, secured with furring strips and screws at the perimeter rather than staples or tape, which fail fast in Florida humidity and sun.

A thorough crew also photographs every opening before and after boarding, which becomes part of the documentation your insurance adjuster will ask for. This is also the point where a contractor can flag secondary risk, like a soaked ceiling that's about to sag, before it becomes a bigger job. If water has already reached drywall or insulation, that typically moves into a separate [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) scope.

## How Fast Should Board-Up and Tarping Happen After Storm or Fire Damage?

Board-up and tarping should happen as soon as it is safe to access the property, generally within a few hours of the damage occurring, because every hour an opening sits exposed increases the risk of secondary water damage and mold growth.

The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org), the industry body behind the S500 water damage standard, treats rapid response as the single biggest factor in whether a loss stays a repair or becomes a full reconstruction. A boarded window that sits open overnight during a Central Florida thunderstorm can turn a $400 glass repair into a five-figure drywall and mold job. For fire damage specifically, board-up often happens the same day the fire department releases the scene, before soot and standing water from suppression efforts sit any longer than necessary. ## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover Emergency Board-Up and Tarping in Lakeland?

Most Florida homeowners and commercial property policies include a duty-to-mitigate clause, which typically covers reasonable temporary repairs like board-up and tarping, since insurers would rather pay for a tarp now than a collapsed ceiling later.

That said, coverage specifics vary by carrier and policy language, so keep every receipt and photo from the board-up step. ## What Should You Do Before the Board-Up Crew Arrives?

Before a crew arrives, photograph every damaged opening from inside and outside, shut off electrical circuits to any wet area, and move valuables and electronics away from exposed windows or roof damage.

A few other things help the process move faster:

- Don't climb onto a damaged roof yourself. Compromised decking and wet shingles are a serious fall risk, even for a quick look.
- Note the time you discovered the damage. Insurers often ask when the loss occurred versus when mitigation started.
- If the damage came from a storm event, keep an eye on local weather tracking from [NOAA](https://www.noaa.gov) so you know whether a second system is expected before permanent repairs can be scheduled.
- Don't throw away damaged materials until the adjuster has had a chance to review them, unless they pose a safety hazard.

If the loss also involves interior flooding from the same event, that typically needs a separate emergency water removal call alongside the board-up.

A boarded-up or tarped property is not a finished repair, it's a hold.
