## What should you look for in a sewage cleanup company in Thonotosassa?

A qualified sewage cleanup company will have four things you can verify before they ever walk through your door: IICRC certification, a current Florida contractor license, insurance coverage, and a written scope of work before they start. Anything less and you're trusting a guess.

Specifically, ask for or check:

- **IICRC Certified Firm status.** This means the company's technicians are trained to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry protocol that governs how contaminated water is classified, contained, and dried.
- **A Florida contractor license you can verify.** You can look up any Florida license directly through the state at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/) rather than taking a company's word for it.
- **Proof of insurance**, so you're not liable if someone gets hurt handling contaminated materials in your home.
- **A written scope before work starts**, not a verbal estimate that changes once the walls are already open.
- **A real plan for disposal**, since sewage cleanup almost always means removing porous materials (carpet, drywall, insulation) rather than drying them in place.

A company that can't answer these questions clearly on the phone is not the company you want handling black water in an occupied home.

## Why does Category 3 water classification matter for sewage cleanup?

A sewage backup is Category 3 water, also called black water, which means it's grossly contaminated and requires removal and disposal of affected porous materials rather than just drying. This classification isn't a technicality: it determines the entire scope of the job.

Under the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), any backup from a sewer line or a floor drain beyond the trap, or a toilet overflow carrying feces, is automatically Category 3. That's different from, say, a burst supply line, which starts as clean (Category 1) water. A crew that tries to dry Category 3-affected carpet pad or drywall instead of removing it is cutting corners that show up later as odor or microbial growth.

A properly run Category 3 job includes containment to keep contamination from spreading to unaffected rooms, removal of porous materials that can't be sanitized, antimicrobial treatment of the structure, and PPE for the crew, including gloves, respirators, and often full suits. If a company's proposal skips straight to "we'll dry it out," that's a red flag.

## Will homeowners insurance cover sewage cleanup?

Most standard homeowners policies do not automatically cover sewer or drain backup damage; coverage typically requires a separate water backup (sewer/sump) endorsement added to the policy. This surprises a lot of homeowners who assume any water damage inside the home is covered the same way.

Check your declarations page for a line item specifically naming "water backup" or "sewer and drain backup" coverage. If it's there, document everything with photos before cleanup starts and keep every receipt: the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster) notes that prompt documentation and mitigation protect a claim. If that endorsement isn't on your policy, the cleanup cost is likely out of pocket, which makes it even more important to get a written scope and a fair price up front rather than an open-ended invoice.

A company that's used to working with Florida carriers should be able to tell you, in plain language, what's typically covered and what documentation the adjuster will want. That's a service piece, not just a cleaning piece.
