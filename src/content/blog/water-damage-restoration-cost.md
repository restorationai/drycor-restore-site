## How much does water damage restoration cost in Tampa Bay?

In the Tampa Bay area, water damage restoration typically costs between $3.50 and $7.50 per square foot for mitigation alone, not including any reconstruction. That range widens significantly based on three variables: how much area is affected, what category of water caused the damage, and how long drying takes. Florida's average relative humidity runs 70 to 90 percent for much of the year, which means drying cycles that take 3 days in a dry climate can take 5 to 7 days here, adding equipment-day costs to every job.

Here is a realistic breakdown by loss size:

| Loss Scenario | Typical Mitigation Cost |
|---|---|
| Single room, clean water (Cat 1) | $1,500 - $4,500 |
| Single room, gray water (Cat 2) | $2,500 - $6,000 |
| Single room, sewage (Cat 3) | $4,000 - $9,000 |
| Whole floor (1,000-1,500 sq ft), Cat 1 | $5,000 - $12,000 |
| Whole floor, Cat 2 or Cat 3 | $8,000 - $18,000 |
| Whole-home flood or storm surge | $12,000 - $35,000+ |

These are mitigation figures only: water extraction, structural drying, antimicrobial treatment, and moisture documentation. Reconstruction (drywall replacement, flooring, cabinetry) is a separate line item and can double the total on a large loss.

## What factors push the cost up or down?

Five things move the number more than anything else.

**Water category.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500) defines three categories of water. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line or appliance. Category 2, also called gray water, comes from dishwashers, washing machines, or toilet overflow without solid waste. Category 3, also called black water, includes sewage, floodwater from outside, or any water that has sat long enough to become grossly contaminated. Cat 2 and Cat 3 require antimicrobial treatment, more aggressive demolition of porous materials, and stricter containment, all of which cost more.

**Affected materials.** Concrete and tile dry faster and cheaper than hardwood flooring, drywall, and insulation. When water reaches wall cavities or subfloor assemblies, crews have to open walls and remove material to dry the structure properly. Skipping that step leads to hidden mold within 24 to 72 hours in Florida's climate.

**Response time.** The faster mitigation starts, the less secondary damage accumulates. A loss caught in the first few hours may stay a Class 1 or Class 2 drying job. The same loss discovered 48 hours later may require full wall demolition and mold remediation, which can add $3,000 to $10,000 to the total.

**Florida humidity.** This is the factor most national cost estimates miss. Tampa Bay's ambient humidity forces restoration crews to run low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers longer than in drier climates. Each equipment day adds roughly $75 to $150 per unit to the job cost. A job that uses four dehumidifiers for 7 days instead of 4 days adds $1,200 to $1,800 in equipment cost alone before labor.

**Affected square footage and building type.** Slab-on-grade construction (very common in Hillsborough and Pinellas counties) traps moisture under flooring with no crawl space for airflow. That often requires specialty drying mats or floor drying systems, which add cost but protect the slab and flooring above it.

## What does the restoration process actually include?

A properly scoped water damage job follows a defined sequence. Understanding the phases helps you read an estimate and know what you're paying for.

1. **Emergency extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This is billed by the hour or as a flat mobilization fee, typically $500 to $1,500.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and pin-type or pinless moisture meters to document the full extent of saturation. This step determines what gets removed and what stays.
3. **Controlled demolition.** Wet drywall, insulation, flooring, and baseboards that cannot be dried in place are removed. This is one of the largest cost variables on a job.
4. **Structural drying.** Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run continuously, typically 3 to 7 days in Florida conditions. Psychrometric readings (temperature, humidity, dew point) are logged daily to confirm drying progress.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Applied to all affected structural surfaces, especially on Cat 2 or Cat 3 losses. Required by the IICRC S500 standard for contaminated water events.
6. **Final moisture clearance.** A final moisture reading confirms the structure has returned to acceptable levels before reconstruction begins. This documentation matters for your insurance file.

## Does homeowners insurance cover water damage restoration?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, but exclude gradual leaks and flooding from outside. The distinction matters a great deal for your out-of-pocket cost.

Covered events typically include: burst pipes, appliance failures (washing machine hose, dishwasher line), HVAC condensate overflows, and roof leaks caused by a sudden storm event. Not covered by standard policies: slow leaks behind walls, sewer backup (unless you have a rider), and rising floodwater from outside (that requires a separate NFIP or private flood policy).

Your deductible applies first. In Florida, wind and hurricane deductibles are often a percentage of your dwelling coverage (1 to 5 percent), which can be $5,000 to $15,000 on a mid-range home. For non-hurricane water losses, a flat deductible of $1,000 to $2,500 is more common.

Documentation is what protects your claim. The restoration company's moisture logs, daily psychrometric readings, and photo documentation of all affected areas are what the adjuster uses to validate the scope. ## What should you do right now to protect the cost?

Three actions in the first hour significantly affect both the final bill and your insurance outcome.

First, stop the source. If a supply line or appliance caused the loss, shut off the water at the nearest valve or at the main. A running source that goes unaddressed for an extra hour can double the affected area.

Second, call a restoration company before you call your insurance company. Adjusters appreciate when mitigation is already underway. Waiting for adjuster approval before starting drying is not required and can allow mold to establish, which complicates the claim.

Third, do not run residential fans or a household dehumidifier and call it done. Residential equipment does not have the capacity to dry structural assemblies. It can actually spread contaminated air through the home on a Cat 2 or Cat 3 loss. Professional LGR dehumidifiers pull 80 to 150 pints of water per day; a household unit pulls 30 to 50 pints and cannot handle the dew-point control that structural drying requires.
