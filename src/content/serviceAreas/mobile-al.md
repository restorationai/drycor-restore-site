## Restoration emergencies common in Mobile

Mobile sits on the Gulf Coast, and that location drives most of the calls we get. Hurricane season runs from June through November, and even storms that stay offshore push heavy rain bands and storm surge risk into low-lying parts of the city. Humidity stays high most of the year, which means a water intrusion that would dry out in a week elsewhere can turn into a mold problem here in days rather than weeks. Older roofs and aging plumbing, combined with sudden downpours, are a common source of ceiling leaks, burst pipes, and basement or crawlspace flooding across the area.

## Services we provide in Mobile

Water damage restoration and emergency water removal are frequent calls here, especially after the heavy rain events that roll in off the Gulf. Flood damage restoration and storm damage restoration follow the same pattern: wind-driven rain finds its way through roofing and window flashing that was never built for sustained tropical weather.

Burst pipe cleanup and repair and water heater flood cleanup come up often in homes with plumbing that has been in the ground or the walls for decades. Sewage cleanup and sanitization is its own category, since Mobile's older sewer infrastructure and heavy rainfall can combine to cause backups that need to be handled as a biohazard, not just a wet mess.

Fire damage restoration and smoke damage restoration cover the structural and odor side of a fire loss, while contents restoration and storage protects furniture, documents, and belongings while the structure dries or rebuilds. Mold inspection and testing, along with mold remediation, matter more here than in drier climates because the coastal humidity gives mold a head start the moment materials stay wet. For buildings that need more than drying out, reconstruction services, renovations and general contracting, and emergency board-up and tarping round out the work, with commercial, industrial, and large loss response available for property managers and businesses dealing with bigger footprints.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Mobile

Much of Mobile's housing stock dates from the early to mid-20th century, with wood-frame construction, slab-on-grade or pier-and-beam foundations, and plumbing that ranges from original cast iron and galvanized pipe to more recent PEX replacements. Homes built before the 1970s may still have lead-based paint or asbestos-containing materials in flooring, siding, or insulation, which affects how a restoration crew can safely demo and dry a space. The area's sandy, low-lying soil and proximity to Mobile Bay mean a high water table in many neighborhoods, so crawlspaces and low foundations are prone to moisture intrusion even without a major storm.

Structural repair or rebuild work typically requires a permit through the City of Mobile's building and permitting office, and flood-zone designations near the bay and surrounding waterways can add requirements for elevation or flood-resistant materials during reconstruction. A restoration scope in this city has to account for both the age of the building and its exposure to water from above and below.
