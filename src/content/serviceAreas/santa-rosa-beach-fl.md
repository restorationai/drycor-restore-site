## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Santa Rosa Beach

Much of Santa Rosa Beach was built from the 1980s onward, with wood-frame construction over slab-on-grade or elevated pier and piling foundations closer to the water, the latter required where flood-zone rules call for living space above base flood elevation. Supply lines in homes from that era are typically copper or early PEX, and drain lines are PVC, though older sections may still carry original cast iron. Sandy soil drains quickly but sits above a water table that rises with seasonal rain, which is why crawlspace moisture and foundation-adjacent ponding are common even on lots that look well-graded. Walton County's building department handles permitting for structural repair, and work that touches framing, roofing, or elevation after storm damage generally triggers a permit review, particularly on properties inside mapped flood zones. Wind load requirements under Florida's building code are a real factor in how a roof or envelope repair gets scoped here, and HOA architectural review is common in many of the planned coastal communities.
