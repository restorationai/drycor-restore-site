## Restoration emergencies common in Fairhope

Fairhope sits on the bluff above Mobile Bay, which means two things work against local homes at once: tropical storm systems that track up the Gulf from June through November, and a water table that sits close to the surface in low-lying lots near the bay. Heavy rain bands and storm surge push water into crawlspaces and ground-floor living areas faster than gravity alone would suggest, and the region's humidity gives mold a head start once materials stay wet. Older homes near downtown with original wood siding and single-pane windows are especially prone to wind-driven rain intrusion during named storms, while newer construction closer to Highway 98 deals more often with roof and gutter failures during heavy summer thunderstorms.

Fairhope's downtown and bluff-side neighborhoods carry a mix of early-1900s cottages and bungalows alongside newer infill construction, which means a restoration crew has to plan for both older cast-iron and galvanized plumbing and the copper or PEX systems found in more recent remodels.
