## Why Lake Magdalene Properties See Water Damage

Most homes around Lake Magdalene were built from the 1960s through the 1980s on concrete slabs, often with terrazzo or tile set directly over the slab and carpet laid on top of that. When a pipe fails under that slab, or when storm water backs up against the foundation, moisture has nowhere to go but up through the flooring and into baseboards and drywall. Add in Central Florida's humid subtropical climate, with afternoon thunderstorms nearly every day in summer and a hurricane season that runs June through November, and a wet subfloor that would dry out in a week up north can stay damp for much longer here if it isn't pulled apart and dried properly. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lake Magdalene

We start by identifying the water category and extracting standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, which matters more in slab homes where water can pool under cabinetry and baseboards rather than draining to a lower level. From there we pull back flooring and baseboards as needed to get airflow to the slab itself, since concrete holds moisture longer than wood subfloor and reads deceptively dry at the surface while still saturated a few millimeters down. Drying is set up and monitored to the IICRC S500 standard, with moisture meters checking the slab, drywall, and framing daily rather than guessing based on how a room feels. In lake-adjacent homes we also check attic and soffit areas for wind-driven rain intrusion, which is a common secondary damage source during Florida's storm season that homeowners don't expect to find.
