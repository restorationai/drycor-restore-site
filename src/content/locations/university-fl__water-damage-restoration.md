University sits on flat, low-lying terrain between Tampa and Thonotosassa where the water table sits close to the surface year-round, and that single fact changes how water damage behaves here compared to higher, better-drained parts of Hillsborough County.

## Why University Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes and apartment buildings in the University area are slab-on-grade, a construction style common across central Florida since the 1960s and 70s. When a water heater fails or a supply line bursts, there's no crawlspace to catch and vent the moisture, so water spreads laterally under baseboards and into wall cavities before anyone notices a problem at the surface. Add in Florida's summer thunderstorm pattern, where an afternoon downpour can drop an inch of rain in under thirty minutes, and roof or window intrusion becomes its own category of loss separate from plumbing failures. The area's mix of owner-occupied homes and rental housing serving the University of South Florida population also means a lot of properties sit vacant for stretches between leases, which is exactly when a slow leak under a sink or behind a washing machine goes undetected longest.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in University

We start with extraction, pulling standing water off tile, vinyl, and carpet with truck-mounted or portable pumps before it has time to migrate further into the slab or wall assembly. On slab construction, we pay close attention to moisture readings at the base of walls, since concrete can hold water for days and release it slowly back into drywall and baseboards even after the visible surface looks dry. We set drying equipment calibrated to Florida's humidity, because running standard air movers in a house with 70 percent ambient humidity outside does little good if the HVAC system isn't also pulling its share of the load. Structural drying here typically runs longer than in drier climates for that reason, and we track it daily with moisture meters rather than guessing based on a fixed schedule, documenting readings as we go so the drying log holds up if your insurance adjuster asks for it.

## University Insurance & HOA Coordination

A fair number of properties in and around University are rentals or investment homes, which means the insurance conversation often involves a landlord policy, a property manager, and sometimes a tenant who isn't on the policy at all. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings from the first visit so whoever is filing the claim, owner or managing agent, has what they need without a second site visit. Where a homeowners association is involved, we coordinate directly with property management on scheduling equipment pickup and any required drywall or flooring repairs so the unit stays within community guidelines during the restoration.

## Local note

Because so much of University's housing stock sits on a slab with the water table close beneath it, we've learned to check for moisture wicking up through the slab itself, not just water coming down from a fixture or roof. A loss that looks contained to one room can show elevated slab moisture readings several feet past the visible water line, which is why we map moisture across the whole affected area before calling drying complete rather than relying on how the floor looks or feels.
