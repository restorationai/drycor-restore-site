## Why Pensacola Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Emergencies

Escambia County's hurricane season runs roughly June through November, and Pensacola takes a direct hit from Gulf systems more often than most Florida coastal cities its size. High winds peel back roofing underlayment and shingles, and storm surge pressure can blow out ground-floor windows and doors even on homes that never flood. Add in the area's older downtown housing stock, with wood-frame construction and original single-pane windows in some of the historic districts, and you get structures that are more vulnerable to wind-load failure than newer builds out toward the county line. Fires are the other driver: a kitchen or electrical fire that compromises an exterior wall or roof section leaves the same problem as storm damage, an open building that needs to be weather-tight fast.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Pensacola

We start with a walk of the exterior to identify every compromised opening, not just the obvious one, since wind damage in Pensacola often creates secondary stress fractures in nearby window frames or soffits that aren't visible from the street. Plywood is cut and fitted to window and door openings, fastened to framing rather than just trim, since gulf-coast wind gusts will tear loose anything screwed into soft material. Roof tarping uses heavy-gauge poly sheeting battened down with furring strips, not just nailed at the edges, because sustained coastal wind will work a poorly secured tarp loose within days. Everything is photographed before and after for your insurance file. If the structure has fire damage, we coordinate the board-up with the broader restoration scope so the secured openings don't need to be reopened twice.

## Pensacola Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most Pensacola homeowner policies treat emergency board-up and tarping as mitigation, which carriers generally expect you to do regardless of whether a full claim is approved yet; failing to secure the property can affect how a subsequent claim is handled. We document the pre-loss condition, the emergency measures taken, and materials used so that cost can be submitted alongside the larger claim. For properties inside a homeowners' association or a historic overlay district downtown, exterior alterations, even temporary ones like plywood over a window, can be subject to association notice requirements; we keep the work visually minimal and temporary-grade so it doesn't conflict with those rules while the permanent repair is arranged.
