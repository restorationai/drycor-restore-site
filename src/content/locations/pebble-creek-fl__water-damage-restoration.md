## Why Pebble Creek Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes in this part of Hillsborough County were built on concrete slab foundations with concrete block or block-and-stucco (CBS) walls, a construction style that handles Florida heat well but behaves differently than wood framing once it gets wet. Water doesn't just soak into drywall here, it tracks along the slab, wicks up through block cores, and can travel several feet from the original source before it ever shows as a stain. Add in the region's pattern of sudden afternoon thunderstorms during the wet season and the ambient humidity that rarely drops below the point where air alone can dry a structure, and you get a climate where standing water or a hidden leak turns into a mold conversation faster than it would in a drier state. Aging supply lines, water heaters, and AC condensate drains are common culprits we see across this corridor, especially in homes built before more recent plumbing codes were adopted.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Pebble Creek

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable units so carpet pad, baseboards, and subfloor aren't sitting in it any longer than necessary. From there we document moisture readings in the slab and block cavities themselves, not just the visible surfaces, because in CBS construction the core of the wall can stay wet long after the drywall face reads dry. Drying equipment is placed to account for that lag: industrial dehumidifiers paired with air movers positioned to pull trapped moisture out of block cores and slab edges, not just circulate air across a room. We monitor and log readings daily and don't pull equipment until the structure meets a dry standard consistent with IICRC S500 guidelines, not just until it looks dry.

## Local note

One thing that trips up homeowners here: a concrete slab that feels cool and dry to the touch can still be holding moisture underneath sealed flooring like tile, vinyl plank, or laminate.
