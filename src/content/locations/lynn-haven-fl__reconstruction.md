## Why Lynn Haven Properties Need Full Reconstruction, Not Just Repairs

A lot of reconstruction work we take on in Lynn Haven traces back to compounding damage rather than a single event. A roof that lost shingles during Hurricane Michael in 2018 and got a temporary patch can still be letting moisture into the deck and trusses years later. Add a kitchen fire, a slow plumbing leak, or storm surge from a tropical system moving up the Gulf, and what looked like a cosmetic repair job turns into stripping walls to the studs and rebuilding framing, subfloor, and finishes. Bay County's humidity means any structural lumber or drywall left wet for more than a day or two is already supporting mold growth, which changes the scope of the job before our crew even starts demolition.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Lynn Haven

We start with a structural assessment to separate what's salvageable from what has to come out, then document everything with photos for your adjuster before any demolition begins. Demolition and debris removal come next, followed by drying and treatment of any remaining structural wood per the IICRC S500 standard if moisture is still a factor. From there we rebuild: framing, electrical and plumbing rough-in where needed, insulation, drywall, and finish work, matching materials to what the home originally had where that's practical. Every structural phase gets permitted and inspected through the local building department before we close up walls, which matters more in Lynn Haven than in cities without a recent history of storm-driven code enforcement.

## Insurance and Permitting Coordination

Most of the reconstruction jobs we handle in Lynn Haven run through a homeowner's or commercial property policy, often with a wind or flood rider that complicates the claim. We itemize labor and materials separately from the demolition and mitigation phases so your adjuster can see exactly what's structural rebuild versus what was cleanup, and we hold our license (#CBC1253966) current for any permitted work the city requires. If your property sits in a flood-prone section near the Econfina or Big Cove Bayou drainage areas, we also flag whether elevation or floodproofing requirements apply before we rebuild, since that can change the scope and the timeline.

## Local note

One thing we watch closely on Lynn Haven jobs: homes built before the mid-2000s wind code updates often have roof-to-wall connections that don't meet current hurricane-tie standards. When we're already reconstructing a roof or exterior wall section, we flag this to the homeowner and their adjuster early, because retrofitting the connection while the wall is open is far cheaper than doing it as a separate project later, and it's often required anyway to pass final inspection.
