## Why Pensacola Properties See Fire Damage Issues

Pensacola's building stock mixes older wood-frame homes near the downtown core with newer construction built to the Florida Building Code's high-velocity hurricane and wind-borne debris standards. Both come with fire risks of their own. Older homes often carry original knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring that wasn't built for modern electrical loads, and salt-laden Gulf air accelerates corrosion on panels, outlets, and junction boxes, which raises the odds of an electrical fire over time. Afternoon thunderstorms are routine here from late spring through early fall, and lightning strikes on roofs and attics are a recurring cause of structure fires we see across Escambia County. Add in power surges during hurricane season, and Pensacola properties face a fire risk profile shaped as much by weather as by wiring age.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Pensacola

We start with board-up and tarping to secure the structure, since a fire-damaged roof or window left open invites rain within days in this climate, not weeks. From there our crews assess char depth, soot type, and structural integrity before removing damaged materials. Smoke and soot cleanup gets handled differently depending on what burned: protein residue from a kitchen fire behaves differently than the dry, powdery soot from a wood-frame structure fire, and each calls for a different cleaning agent and technique. Because Gulf Coast humidity pushes moisture back into drying materials almost as fast as we remove it, we monitor structural drying more closely here than we would in a drier region, often extending dehumidification time to keep soot residue from reactivating and re-depositing. Odor control, air scrubbing, and reconstruction follow once the structure reads dry and clean.
