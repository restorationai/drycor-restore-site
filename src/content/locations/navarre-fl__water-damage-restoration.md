## Why Navarre Properties See Water Damage Issues

A lot of Navarre's housing stock went up during the coastal growth boom of the 1990s and 2000s, much of it slab-on-grade construction suited to the flat, sandy terrain. Slab homes hide plumbing lines under concrete, so a slow leak under a kitchen or bathroom slab can go undetected for weeks, surfacing only as discolored flooring or a musty smell near baseboards. Add in the region's hurricane season, which runs June through November, and wind-driven rain finding its way through roof flashing or window seals is a recurring cause of interior water intrusion we see across the county. The sandy soil that makes Navarre's beaches famous also drains quickly after a storm, but it does little to protect a structure once water has already gotten inside.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Navarre

We start with extraction, pulling standing water from flooring and carpet pad using truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much water is involved and how accessible the space is. From there we move into structural drying: moisture mapping with meters to find out how far water has traveled into drywall, subfloor, and framing, followed by placement of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the space. In slab homes, we pay close attention to baseboards and the bottom few inches of drywall, since moisture wicking up from a saturated slab can keep a wall damp long after the visible water is gone. Drying is monitored daily and documented against the IICRC S500 standard until materials read dry, not just dry to the touch.
