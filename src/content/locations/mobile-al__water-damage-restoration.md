DRYCOR RESTORE answers water damage calls in Mobile around the clock, and our crews work from the same IICRC S500 drying standard whether the loss is a burst supply line or storm-driven flooding off Mobile Bay.

## Why Mobile Properties See Water Damage Issues

Mobile sits low relative to the bay and the Mobile River, and that geography shapes how water behaves once it gets inside a structure. Older homes near the downtown core, many built on pier-and-beam or shallow crawl space foundations, tend to trap moisture underneath the living space long after a leak or flood event has visibly receded. Newer construction on slab foundations has the opposite problem: water has nowhere to go but up into drywall and baseboards, where it wicks steadily through the gypsum. Add in a subtropical climate where relative humidity outdoors regularly sits in the 80s and 90s during summer, and a wet building rarely dries on its own, even with windows open and fans running. Afternoon thunderstorms are routine from June through September, and tropical systems moving through the Gulf can push wind-driven rain into roof penetrations, window flashing, and older stucco walls that were never rated for sustained saturation.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Mobile

The first step is always extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable extraction units before it has a chance to wick further into flooring and subfloor material. From there we moisture-map the structure with meters and thermal imaging, because in Mobile's climate a wall or ceiling can read dry to the touch on the surface while the cavity behind it stays saturated for days. Structural drying equipment, air movers and commercial dehumidifiers, gets placed based on that mapping rather than a generic room-by-room guess. We monitor daily and adjust: a drying plan calibrated for a dry Alabama winter looks different than one built for a humid August week, and crews here treat that difference as standard practice rather than an afterthought. Once moisture content returns to baseline for the material type, we document the readings for the insurance file and begin any reconstruction the loss requires, from drywall and flooring to full room rebuilds.

## Mobile Insurance & Storm Claims

Water damage claims in Mobile often get complicated by the line between a covered water loss and an excluded flood event, especially when a claim follows a named storm. A pipe that bursts inside the home is typically a standard water damage claim; water that enters from rising surface water or storm surge usually falls under separate flood coverage, and carriers scrutinize that distinction closely in Gulf Coast markets. We photograph and document the source of water before extraction begins, which gives your adjuster a clearer record of how the loss actually occurred. For Mobile County permits required on larger reconstruction scopes, our team coordinates the inspection schedule so drying and rebuild timelines don't stall waiting on paperwork.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Water Damage

Given how much ambient moisture Mobile air carries for most of the year, we lean on higher-capacity desiccant and LGR (low grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers rather than standard units, since standard equipment can struggle to pull humidity down when outdoor air infiltrates a work area. Antimicrobial treatment gets applied to exposed framing and subfloor as a standard precaution anywhere drying extends past 48 hours, which is common here given baseline humidity levels. Where water damage involves older plaster or lath construction, found in a number of Mobile's historic homes, extraction happens carefully to avoid further cracking, and drying times get extended rather than rushed.

## Local note

Mobile's summer humidity rarely gives drying equipment a break, even overnight. Technicians working here routinely run dehumidifiers longer than the equipment's rated drying time charts suggest, because ambient moisture re-enters a work area through small gaps faster than it would in a drier inland climate. Crews that don't account for that tend to call a job "dry" before the structure actually is.
