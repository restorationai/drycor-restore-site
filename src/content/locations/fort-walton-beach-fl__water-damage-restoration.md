## Why Fort Walton Beach Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes and condos along this stretch of the Gulf Coast are built slab-on-grade, which is efficient for hurricane-resistant construction but unforgiving when a supply line fails or a washing machine hose lets go. Water has nowhere to drain and instead sits against the slab, wicking into drywall, baseboards, and cabinetry from the bottom up. Add in Okaloosa County's thick summer humidity and frequent afternoon thunderstorms, and a slow leak behind a wall can go undetected for weeks because the surface finish stays looking fine while the framing underneath stays wet. Coastal properties also deal with corroded plumbing and fixtures at a faster rate than inland homes, since salt air accelerates metal fatigue in supply lines and water heater fittings.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Fort Walton Beach

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted equipment before it has a chance to spread further under cabinetry or flooring. From there we use moisture meters to map how far the water has traveled into drywall, subfloor, and framing, because in slab construction the visible wet line on a wall rarely tells the whole story. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed to pull moisture out of the materials that actually hold it rather than just drying the room air. We monitor and log moisture readings daily until materials return to a dry standard, then document everything for the insurance file.
