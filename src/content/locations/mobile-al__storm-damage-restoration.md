## Why Mobile Properties See Storm Damage So Often

Mobile sits on the Gulf Coast in a corridor that tracks more tropical activity than almost anywhere else in the country, and the hurricane season from June through November brings a mix of storm surge risk near Mobile Bay, torrential rain bands, and tornadoes spun off from the outer edges of larger systems. The region's clay-heavy soil doesn't drain quickly, so standing water around foundations is common even after storms that don't make national news. Add in a tree canopy full of mature live oaks and tall pines, both prone to limb failure and windthrow in saturated ground, and you get a steady stream of roof punctures, siding damage, and fence and carport collapses across the county. Older homes built on pier-and-beam foundations, common in Mobile's historic neighborhoods, are also more vulnerable to wind uplift than newer slab construction, which changes how we approach securing a structure after a storm passes.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Mobile

The first visit is about stopping further loss: emergency board-up over broken windows or exterior walls, tarping on compromised roof sections, and extraction if wind-driven rain has gotten into the living space. From there we document everything with photos and moisture readings before any debris moves, which matters when a claim includes both wind and water components, as most hurricane and tropical storm claims in this area do. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, and because Gulf Coast humidity stays high even after a storm clears, we run dehumidification longer than drying jobs in drier climates typically require. Once materials read dry, our reconstruction crews handle the rebuild, whether that's a section of roof decking, drywall, or full room reconstruction after tree impact.

## Local note

Mobile's older downtown and midtown housing stock includes a lot of heart pine framing and original brick veneer, both of which behave differently under water intrusion than modern construction.
