## Why Crystal Springs Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes out here are slab-on-grade, built across several decades, and many sit on private wells with septic systems rather than municipal service. That combination changes how water damage shows up. A failed well pressure tank or a cracked supply line under the slab can run for hours before anyone notices, because there is no city water meter spinning to give an early warning. Add in Pasco County's karst geology, where soil subsidence and minor settling are common, and you get slab cracks that let groundwater migrate into the home during the region's heavy summer thunderstorm season. Flat, low-lying lots in this part of the county also drain slower than you'd expect, so storm runoff can pool against foundations for a day or two after a front passes through.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Crystal Springs

We start by identifying whether the water is coming from inside the structure (supply line, water heater, fixture) or from outside (storm intrusion, septic backup, grading issue), because the mitigation plan and the insurance paperwork both depend on that answer. Extraction comes first, with truck-mount or portable pumps depending on how much standing water we're dealing with. On slab homes we pay close attention to what's happening under tile and laminate, since trapped moisture between the slab and flooring can sit for weeks and read misleadingly dry on the surface. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers positioned based on psychrometric readings we take room by room, not a one-size-fits-all setup. We also check crawlspace vents and under-house insulation when the home has one, since wet batt insulation left in place is one of the most common reasons drying times run long out here.
