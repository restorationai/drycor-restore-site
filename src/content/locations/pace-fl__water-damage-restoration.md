## Why Pace Properties See Water Damage Issues

Pace sits in a fast-growing stretch of Santa Rosa County where newer subdivisions and older rural properties share the same slab-on-grade construction common throughout the Florida Panhandle. Slab foundations don't allow for a crawlspace buffer, so when a water heater fails or a supply line under the slab develops a pinhole leak, the water has nowhere to go but up through the flooring and into walls. Add in the area's sandy soil and a water table that sits close to the surface for much of the year, and yard drainage problems turn into foundation and interior moisture problems faster than homeowners expect. Heavy summer thunderstorms and the occasional tropical system moving up from the Gulf also push wind-driven rain into soffits, attic vents, and window flashing that was never built to handle sustained saturation.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Pace

We start with moisture mapping, using meters and thermal imaging to find water that's migrated past what's visible, which matters in slab homes where a leak under one room can travel and surface somewhere else entirely. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water from flooring and padding before it has a chance to wick further into subfloor or baseboards. From there we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's size and the material affected, because drywall dries on a different timeline than the engineered wood flooring common in a lot of Pace's newer construction. We monitor daily and document moisture readings throughout, drying structures to the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing at when a space is actually dry. Only once readings confirm dry conditions do we move to repairs, whether that's replacing baseboards or coordinating larger reconstruction.

## Pace Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most water damage claims in this area come down to how quickly the loss gets documented and how clearly the cause is established, since carriers often ask whether a leak was sudden and accidental versus a long-term maintenance issue. We check the attic and HVAC drain line as a matter of course on any ceiling water damage call in this area, since treating it as a roof problem when the real source is mechanical means the damage just comes back.
