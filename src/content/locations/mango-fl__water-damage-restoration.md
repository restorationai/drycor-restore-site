## Why Mango Properties See Water Damage Issues

Mango sits in unincorporated Hillsborough County, where the water table runs high for most of the year and afternoon thunderstorms are a near-daily event from June through September. Slab-on-grade construction, the dominant foundation style across this part of central Florida, means water from a failed washing machine hose or slow slab leak has nowhere to go but sideways under flooring and baseboards. We also see a good number of manufactured and mobile homes in the area, where water intrusion travels fast along belly board and insulation that's much harder to dry in place than open-cavity wall framing. Add in roof and gutter systems that weren't built for sustained tropical downpours, and a single bad storm can push water into places a homeowner won't notice until the baseboards start to swell.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Mango

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable units depending on how far a crew has to reach into the structure. From there we check moisture content in the slab, drywall, and any cabinetry or trim that's wicked water, because in Florida's humidity a surface that feels dry to the touch can still be saturated two inches deeper. Air movers and dehumidifiers go in next, sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected rooms rather than a flat count, and we track drying progress daily against the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing when the job is done. On slab homes especially, we pay close attention to the area directly under flooring, since trapped moisture there is what eventually turns into a mold problem if drying stops too early.

## Local note

A lot of Mango properties, like much of unincorporated Hillsborough County, run on septic rather than municipal sewer. That changes how we think about any water loss that touches a bathroom or laundry area: we check whether the source is a clean supply line or something tied back to the septic system before we start extraction, because the drying plan and any required disinfection step are different depending on which one it is. It's a detail that gets missed by crews who aren't used to working outside the city sewer grid.
