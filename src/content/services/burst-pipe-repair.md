We answer 24/7 for pipe burst emergencies, and the first hour matters more for this type of loss than almost any other: water under pressure finds its way into subfloor, insulation, and wall cavities fast, and the clock on mold growth starts the moment the water stops moving.

## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

A pipe break is really two problems stacked on top of each other: the plumbing failure itself, and the water damage it caused before anyone shut off the supply. Cleanup starts with locating and stopping the source, then moves into extraction, drying, and repair of whatever the water touched on its way out. Depending on where the break happened, that can mean pulling baseboards and drilling weep holes in drywall to let wall cavities dry, lifting sections of laminate or engineered flooring that has already started to cup, or running air movers and dehumidifiers under a kitchen cabinet bank for several days. Pressurized water line breaks tend to travel further and faster than a slow drain leak, so the visible wet spot on the ceiling or floor is rarely the full extent of it. Equipment typically includes submersible pumps for standing water, truck-mount or portable extraction units, axial air movers, and low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers, with moisture meters used throughout to confirm materials are actually drying rather than just looking dry on the surface.

## Our process

1. **Locate and isolate the source.** Before any water is touched, the active leak gets shut down at the main or the nearest isolation valve so the loss stops growing while work begins.
2. **Extract standing water.** Pumps and extraction units pull free water from floors, carpet pad, and any low points, including crawlspaces where a slab leak may have pooled water before it surfaced.
3. **Open and inspect affected cavities.** Baseboards, drywall, and flooring are selectively opened where moisture readings show saturation behind the surface, which is common with wall-run pipe breaks.
4. **Dry in place and monitor.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are set to IICRC S500 drying specifications, with daily moisture checks on wood framing, subfloor, and drywall until readings return to normal.
5. **Repair and reassemble.** Once materials test dry, affected drywall, flooring, cabinetry, or trim is repaired or replaced, and the plumbing fix itself is confirmed before cavities are closed back up.

## What separates a good burst pipe response from a bad one

The most common mistake on a pipe break is treating it like a surface spill instead of a pressurized-water event. A crew that only mops visible water and sets a couple of fans without checking wall cavities or subfloor with a moisture meter will often leave hidden saturation behind, which shows up as a musty smell or soft flooring weeks later. Adjusters look closely at moisture documentation on these claims: readings taken at the start, daily during drying, and at completion, because that log is what proves the structure was actually dried rather than assumed dry. Another frequent miss is failing to check for water that traveled along top plates or down through floor joist bays into a room that wasn't even near the original pipe. Good technicians trace the water's path, not just the obvious wet spot, and document it with photos before any drywall or flooring comes out.

Costs vary a lot based on how far the water traveled before it was found, what materials were affected, and whether any structural drying or cabinetry replacement is involved.
