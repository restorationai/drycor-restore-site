When the drying equipment leaves and the mold containment comes down, most homeowners discover the real work is just starting: a wall that was cut out to chase water intrusion, a kitchen with subfloor removed after a dishwasher line failed, a bedroom with scorched framing behind drywall that had to come down for fire cleanup. Reconstruction is the phase that turns a stripped-out structure back into a livable, code-compliant space, and it is where a lot of restoration jobs stall because the mitigation crew and the rebuild crew are two different companies that don't talk to each other.

## What Reconstruction Services actually involves

Post-damage reconstruction covers everything between "the damaged materials are gone" and "you can move back in." That might mean replacing a section of drywall and baseboard after a small water loss, or it might mean full structural reconstruction after a fire burned through roof trusses and load-bearing walls. The scope depends entirely on what the mitigation phase removed and what the original structure needs to pass inspection. Work can include framing repair, subfloor and sheathing replacement, drywall and insulation, electrical and plumbing rough-in where those systems were compromised, flooring, trim, cabinetry, and paint. Timelines range from a few days for a single-room repair to several months for a structural rebuild that requires permitting and multiple inspection stages. Because DRYCOR RESTORE holds Florida contractor's license #CBC1253966, we're able to carry a job from the demo stage through final permit sign-off without handing it to a separate general contractor.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work.** We document every damaged material, measure the affected square footage, and write a detailed scope that itemizes labor and materials, separate from the mitigation invoice. This is the document your adjuster will compare against the estimate.
2. **Permits and code review.** Any work touching structural framing, electrical, plumbing, or roofing in Hillsborough County typically requires a permit pulled before work starts. We identify what triggers a permit versus what's cosmetic repair so the job doesn't get flagged mid-build.
3. **Framing and rough-in.** Structural members are sistered or replaced, subfloor is re-sheathed, and electrical or plumbing runs are brought back to code if they were part of the original damage.
4. **Finishing.** Insulation, drywall, taping and texture matching, flooring, trim, and paint are brought back to pre-loss condition or better, matched to existing finishes where the loss was partial.
5. **Final walkthrough and inspection.** We schedule the required inspections, close out permits, and walk the space with you before calling the job complete.

## What separates a good reconstruction response from a bad one

The most common failure point is a mismatch between what the mitigation crew tore out and what the rebuild crew actually replaces. If drywall was cut two feet above the flood line but the adjuster's estimate only covers the visible stain, the homeowner ends up paying out of pocket for the difference, or worse, a contractor rebuilds over a wall cavity that's still holding moisture because nobody checked it with a meter before closing it up. Adjusters also look closely at whether materials and finishes in the estimate actually match what's being installed, not a generic substitute, and whether the contractor pulled the permits the work actually required. A rebuild that skips permitting to save time can create problems at resale, since unpermitted work often surfaces in a home inspection years later. Good reconstruction work means matching flooring transitions, texture patterns, and trim profiles closely enough that the repair isn't obvious from across the room, and verifying moisture levels in framing before it gets covered back up.

## What does Reconstruction Services cost?

Reconstruction costs vary more than almost any other restoration service because the scope ranges from a patch repair to a full structural rebuild. Every loss is different, and DRYCOR RESTORE provides a written scope of work with itemized pricing before any reconstruction begins, so there are no surprises mid-job. Most standard homeowners policies cover reconstruction that results from a covered peril, such as a burst pipe or a fire, but gradual damage or pre-existing deferred maintenance is typically excluded, which is why the documentation from the mitigation phase matters.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Thonotosassa's older housing stock and the humid Gulf Coast climate both affect how reconstruction gets sequenced. In homes built before modern vapor barrier standards, framing that's been wet for even a short period needs to read dry on a moisture meter before it's closed back up, or the drywall and insulation installed over it can set up mold growth within the new wall cavity. During the June through November storm season, permitting and inspection turnaround through the county can slow down as departments manage a higher volume of storm-related rebuild applications, which is worth factoring into a project timeline if your loss happens during peak season.
